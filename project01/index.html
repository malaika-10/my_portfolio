I retained the rickshaw as the central viewpoint because it connects the experience to Rawalpindi’s everyday life while giving the journey a continuous setting. BUILDING THE WORLD I modelled the architecture, rickshaw and environmental assets in Blender for the original animation. Narrow lanes, aged facades, roadside stalls, warm lighting and winter fog helped communicate the atmosphere of Androon Rawalpindi. The rickshaw interior establishes the passenger’s viewpoint, while details such as kettles, shop signs and worn surfaces connect the environment to everyday cultural practices. These existing assets form the visual foundation for the VR extension. The interaction prototype communicates the proposed screens, choices and journey structure. 3D models, environment, original animation and interaction prototype. REFLECTION AND NEXT STEPS Extending this project has changed how I think about storytelling. In animation, I control the camera and sequence. In an interactive experience, I must also consider what users notice, what they choose and how the environment responds. The prototype defines the intended experience, but it does not yet establish its usability or comfort in VR. After Unity implementation, I plan to evaluate whether users understand the controls, recognise opportunities to stop and ask questions, and can explore comfortably. It will address readable interfaces, subtitles, sound controls and motion comfort. I also want to review the stories with local contributors so that the experience represents multiple perspectives on Rawalpindi. DESIGNING BEYOND THE FRAME FURTHER DEVELOPMENT
